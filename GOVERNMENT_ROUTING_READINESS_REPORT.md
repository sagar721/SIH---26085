# FLOODWATCH Safe Routing — Government Operational Readiness Audit

Scope: **not** a validation of the routing algorithm (that is covered separately in `ROUTING_VALIDATION_REPORT.md`, which already proves the Dijkstra/cost/blocking logic is internally correct). This audit instead asks the operational question a real deployment decision hinges on: **would MCGM, NDRF, or an Emergency Response Team actually trust and use this routing feature during a live flood, and if not, why not.**

Every finding below is grounded in the actual code and data currently in this repository (`frontend/src/lib/routingEngine.ts`, `frontend/src/components/routing/RoutingPanel.tsx`, `frontend/src/api/adapters/MockAdapter.ts`, `data/scripts/download_osm.py`, `data/processed/flood_model/flood_susceptibility_metadata.json`), not generic routing-software risk boilerplate. Where a risk is already mitigated elsewhere in the app (e.g. the resilience-report's offline/fetch-failure banners), that is credited rather than re-listed as open.

## 1. Data Risks

| ID | Failure Mode | Severity | Likelihood | Consequence | Mitigation | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Road network incompleteness | Critical | Certain | `download_osm.py` pulls each pilot zone as an independent ~5.5km bounding-box Overpass query. `ROUTING_VALIDATION_REPORT.md`'s own empirical sweep already found real named points near a zone edge with **zero road connection at all**, even with no flooding — a data-coverage artifact, not a flood result. An agency will hit "no route" for a destination they know is physically reachable and lose confidence in the tool at exactly the wrong moment. | Render the extract's coverage boundary on the map so operators see what's *not* modelled before they rely on it; long-term, re-pull a citywide (not bbox-clipped) extract. | LOW (coverage overlay) / HIGH (citywide data) |
| D2 | Stale road data | High | High | The OSM extract is pulled once, with no re-pull schedule and no vintage indicator anywhere in the routing UI (the app-wide "SNAPSHOT" badge covers rainfall timing, not road-data age). Roads get rerouted, closed, or newly built continuously in a city the size of Mumbai; a route computed against a stale extract can send a vehicle down a road that no longer exists in that configuration. | Stamp the OSM extract's Overpass fetch date, surface it next to the routing panel, and schedule periodic re-pulls. | LOW (badge) / MEDIUM (pipeline) |
| D3 | Missing road closures | Critical | Certain | There is no data source, field, or override mechanism anywhere in `routingEngine.ts` for an officially-known closure (police barricade, debris, an MCGM-ordered detour). During any real flood event, closures happen continuously and are exactly the kind of ground-truth a field agency has and this system has no way to ingest. | A minimal manual "mark this segment blocked" override layer (agency-operated, timestamped, with a reason) feeding the same `blocked` flag the flood-depth logic already uses. | MEDIUM |
| D4 | Missing construction zones | High | High | No construction dataset is fetched by any script in `data/scripts/`. A route can be sent straight into an active dig or barricaded work zone with no warning. | Same override mechanism as D3 (a shared "known obstruction" layer), ideally sourced from MCGM's own works-department feed if one exists. | MEDIUM–HIGH |
| D5 | Missing bridge failures | Critical | Low–Medium | `download_osm.py` and `download_infrastructure.py` both already capture a real `bridge` tag from OSM, but `routingEngine.ts`'s graph builder never reads it — a bridge segment is weighted and traversed identically to an ordinary residential street unless it happens to fall inside a simulated flood polygon. A structurally compromised or collapsed bridge (a real, if rare, monsoon-season risk in Mumbai) would be silently routed across. | Surface the already-collected `bridge` tag as a distinguishable edge property, and route it through the same manual-block override as D3 for a rapid "this bridge is out" action. | LOW (wire the existing tag through) / MEDIUM (override UI) |
| D6 | Missing underpass flooding | Critical | High | `download_infrastructure.py` captures a `tunnel=yes` (underpass) tag for infrastructure **points**, but this is never propagated to road **edges** in `routingEngine.ts`. Underpasses are one of the most consistent, well-documented flood-fatality locations in Mumbai every monsoon, precisely because their depth relative to street grade doesn't show up in a 30m-resolution DEM (D5/F4) — the generic `susceptibility_score` has no way to "see" a below-grade dip. This is a specific, foreseeable, and currently silent gap between what the model can represent and where people actually die. | Cross-reference the underpass tag against road-edge geometry and apply a hard minimum risk floor (or a manual known-waterlogging-point list, which MCGM likely already maintains informally) rather than relying on DEM-derived susceptibility alone for these segments. | MEDIUM |

## 2. Flood Model Risks

| ID | Failure Mode | Severity | Likelihood | Consequence | Mitigation | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | False positives (route marked risky/blocked when actually passable) | Medium | Medium | Simulated flood polygons (`MockAdapter.generateMockFloodPolygons`) are axis-aligned quadrilaterals sized by a depth-driven radius heuristic, not a hydraulic model — they can over-cover an area relative to where water actually pools. Consequence is unnecessary detours, not danger, but repeated over-blocking is also what drives H2 (trust collapse). | Already labeled SIMULATED end-to-end; keep that provenance visible specifically in the "avoided roads" reasons (already done) rather than implying certainty. | LOW (already substantially in place) |
| F2 | False negatives (route marked safe/low-risk when actually flooded) | **Critical** | Medium–High | `flood_susceptibility_metadata.json` itself states `"status": "MODELLED"`, explicitly `"not": ["OBSERVED", "VALIDATED"]` — this project has never checked its own susceptibility layer against a single real flood outcome. A "Safest" route is only as safe as a model that has never been ground-truthed; a road with a locally blocked drain, a factor the elevation/slope/landcover composite doesn't capture, could show LOW modelled risk while genuinely flooded. This is the single highest-consequence failure mode in the entire feature, because "safest route" is the one output most likely to be acted on literally. | The project already has an unexploited validation resource: `data/scripts/sentinel1_process_scene.py` / `sentinel1_water_detection_validation.json` (real SAR-observed water extent) exist in this repo but have not been cross-checked against the susceptibility layer or routing outputs. Running that comparison, even for one historical event, would convert this from "never validated" to "validated against N observed events." Until then, the "NOT FOR OPERATIONAL USE" disclaimer must stay on every route result without exception. | LOW (strengthen disclaimer now) / HIGH (actual validation against Sentinel-1) |
| F3 | Rainfall uncertainty | High | High | Routing risk and blocking both derive from `effectiveRainfallMmHr`, itself sourced from GSMaP satellite rainfall estimates. Satellite quantitative precipitation estimates carry well-documented bias and uncertainty, especially at the sub-hourly, sub-kilometer scale this app operates at — and that uncertainty is not currently propagated into the risk score or disclosed next to routing output specifically (it is discussed for the dashboard generally). | Disclose the rainfall-source uncertainty directly in the routing panel's provenance footer, not only in the general Methodology modal. | LOW |
| F4 | DEM uncertainty | High | High | Susceptibility is built from Copernicus DEM GLO-30 — 30m resolution. A curb, a single blocked culvert, or a shallow local depression (exactly the features that determine real street flooding) are far below what this DEM can resolve. This is structural to the model, not a bug, but a routing decision needs this caveat attached at the point of use. | Same as F3 — surface the DEM-resolution caveat in the routing panel itself, tied specifically to why a "LOW risk" label is a relative model output, not an absolute guarantee. | LOW |
| F5 | Drainage uncertainty | High | Certain | `MockAdapter.computeBaseDepthMeters`'s `drainageBlockage` term is a user-set "what-if" slider, not a measurement — the project's own OSM script notes MCGM's real underground stormwater network is "unpublished" and unavailable to this project. Real drainage state during an actual event (which drains are already clogged, which pumps are running) is genuinely unknown, full stop. | Already correctly labeled SIMULATED; verify this is unambiguous specifically in routing output (it currently is, via the "SIMULATED" flood-depth reason text), not just in the general scenario controls. | LOW |

## 3. Operational Risks

| ID | Failure Mode | Severity | Likelihood | Consequence | Mitigation | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| O1 | Internet failure | **Critical** | High | Routing needs a live fetch of roads/infrastructure/flood GeoJSON and MapLibre's tile requests; there is no offline capability (disclosed as a HIGH-effort gap for the whole app in `RESILIENCE_REPORT.md`, item 2.5). The failure mode is worse for routing specifically than for passive viewing: connectivity is most likely to degrade in exactly the flooded areas an evacuation route needs to serve, and a tool that goes dark under load is worse than no tool if an agency has built a process around it. | Pre-compute and cache route bundles for known critical corridors while connectivity is still available, downloadable in advance of a forecast event. | HIGH |
| O2 | Regression: weakened operational disclaimer | High | Medium | `RESILIENCE_REPORT.md` (item 3.1) recorded an explicit, prominent disclaimer implemented on the (then 100%-mock) routing panel: *"NOT FOR OPERATIONAL USE… Generated at [time]… re-check before departure."* The routing panel was fully rewritten for the real engine, and the current `RouteDetail` footer (`RoutingPanel.tsx:260-264`) keeps the "not for operational use" line but has **dropped the generation timestamp and the explicit "re-check before departure" instruction**, and renders it at 8px in a muted footer rather than as an unmissable banner. This is a genuine finding from this audit, not carried over from a prior report. | Restore the timestamp + re-check language and give it the same visual prominence as the original resilience-report implementation, now on the real (not mock) output where the stakes are actually live. | LOW |
| O3 | Map tile / basemap failure | Medium | Low–Medium | Style-load failure is already detected and shown (`RESILIENCE_REPORT.md` item 2.3), but a route line and endpoints with no basemap context is close to operationally useless for a field responder trying to orient themselves. | Fall back to a lower-fidelity, more cacheable tile source, or a minimal vector-only rendering of just roads/water, when the primary basemap is unreachable. | MEDIUM–HIGH |
| O4 | Delayed / absent live rainfall updates | Critical | Certain | This is not a latency problem — the entire dataset (per the app-wide "SNAPSHOT — NOT LIVE" badge already implemented) is a fixed historical window. Routing therefore cannot reflect real, current conditions at all today; it is a scenario/backtest tool, not a live decision-support system, regardless of how the UI otherwise looks. An agency evaluating this for live command-post use needs this stated as plainly for routing as it is for the rest of the dashboard. | Live GSMaP or IMD feed integration is a prerequisite for any live-use claim; until then, keep the SNAPSHOT badge's visibility unreduced when the routing panel is the active view. | HIGH |
| O5 | Missing/partial datasets specific to routing | Medium | Medium | General fetch failures already report into `useDataHealthStore` (verified in `RealDataAdapter.ts`, every `fetch(...).catch()` calls `reportFailure`), so a hard failure is visible. A softer failure is not: if infrastructure data loads but is merely empty for a zone, `buildInfraInfluence` returns `[]` and the infrastructure-exposure risk term silently becomes zero everywhere — a route can be labeled "safest" while one of its five stated risk inputs (infrastructure exposure) contributed nothing, with no indication to the operator that this happened. | Track a "based on N/5 real risk inputs" indicator, or at minimum flag when the infra-influence set used to build a specific route was empty. | LOW |

## 4. Human Risks

| ID | Failure Mode | Severity | Likelihood | Consequence | Mitigation | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | User misinterpretation of "Safest" | **Critical** | High | The button is literally labeled "Safest" (`RoutingPanel.tsx` `MODE_META`). Under the stress of an active flood event, a field operator reading "Safest" has every reason to interpret it as a certified-safe route rather than "lowest relative modelled risk among three computed alternatives" — especially compounded by F2 (the model itself has never been validated). This is the single highest human-factors risk in the feature because the failure is invisible until it's too late. | Keep "Safest" as the compact label but require a one-time acknowledgment of what it actually means before the first route of a session is shown, and strengthen the always-visible disclaimer per O2. | LOW |
| H2 | Route trust collapse | High | Medium–High | The flip side of H1: once an operator experiences a "no safe route available" for a destination they know is reachable (D1) or later learns a "low risk" route was in fact impassable (F2), a common human response to an imperfect decision-support tool is to stop trusting it entirely — including for the cases where it would have been correct. This is a credibility risk, not a technical one, and it compounds every other item in this report. | The correct mitigation is exactly this project's existing honesty convention — disclosing limitations up front, in place, rather than after a failure in the field — plus an explicit feedback channel for field-observed discrepancies to feed back into the model. | LOW–MEDIUM (process) |
| H3 | Emergency vehicle routing not supported | High | Certain (unsupported today) | `ROAD_CLASS_SPEED_KMH` and the fixed `IMPASSABLE_DEPTH_M = 0.3` in `routingEngine.ts` both assume one generic passenger-vehicle profile. There is no vehicle-class parameter: an NDRF high-clearance vehicle or a fire truck can safely traverse depths well beyond 0.3m and has an effective speed profile (siren/right-of-way) nothing like a private car's, yet the tool has exactly one profile for everyone. For the stated NDRF/Emergency Services deployment audience, this is a direct mismatch between the tool and its intended user. | Add a vehicle-class selector (passenger / ambulance / fire-appliance / high-clearance NDRF) with class-specific impassable-depth thresholds and speed multipliers. | MEDIUM |
| H4 | Panic-mode usability | Medium | High | Origin/destination selection is a plain HTML `<select>` populated with all 168 real named locations per zone, alphabetically sorted, with no search/typeahead and no "use my current location" option (the app has no geolocation integration at all, confirmed by inspection). Scrolling a long unsearchable dropdown is a poor interaction pattern generally and a genuinely bad one under panic, when the fastest possible input is what's needed. | Add a searchable/typeahead location picker and a geolocation-based "start from here" button. | LOW–MEDIUM |

## 5. Disaster Risks

| ID | Failure Mode | Severity | Likelihood | Consequence | Mitigation | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| X1 | City-wide flooding | **Critical** | Certain in the named scenario | Coverage is exactly two ~5.5km pilot zones (Kurla-Sion, Hindmata-Dadar). A real Mumbai deluge event (2005, 2017, 2021-scale) floods across the city, not inside two boxes — everywhere else, this tool has literally nothing to say, and nothing in the current UI makes that boundary visually obvious before an operator tries to route somewhere outside it. | Render the modelled coverage area as a visible boundary layer on the map at all times, with explicit "no data beyond this line" messaging; citywide data acquisition is the real fix. | LOW (coverage overlay) / HIGH (citywide data) |
| X2 | Complete corridor isolation | **Critical** | Confirmed by testing | `ROUTING_VALIDATION_REPORT.md`'s own empirical sweep of ~15 real pairs found 0 of 11 valid-baseline routes showed a graceful partial reroute under a moderate flood scenario — routes went straight from clear to "No safe route available." In a real corridor-isolation event (one arterial floods, cutting off a neighborhood), an agency gets a flat "no route" with no partial guidance (how far can a vehicle safely get, where's the nearest reachable shelter/high ground) — less useful than a human dispatcher with local knowledge would provide in the same situation. | Add a "furthest safely reachable point" fallback mode that routes as far as possible and flags the remaining gap, and integrate a designated shelter/high-ground point layer as a routing target of last resort. | MEDIUM–HIGH |
| X3 | Communication failure | High | High | Already disclosed at the whole-app level (`RESILIENCE_REPORT.md` item 3.3 — no real MCGM/NDRF alerting integration exists). Specific to routing: a computed route cannot be exported or handed off to a vehicle's radio/GPS, nor can a corridor closure discovered by one unit be broadcast to others through this tool. | Add a route export (GPX / shareable link) as a low-effort first step; full dispatch integration is the disclosed longer-term item. | MEDIUM |
| X4 | Power outages | Medium | Medium | Already N/A at the application level generally (`RESILIENCE_REPORT.md` item 5.2). Specific to routing: `ROAD_CLASS_SPEED_KMH` assumes normal traffic-signal operation; during a power outage (common alongside major flooding) real effective road speeds change in ways the static table can't represent, making the already-MODELLED ETA even less reliable exactly when it would be most relied upon. | State this assumption explicitly next to the ETA figure in the routing panel. | LOW |

## What this audit is not

It does not re-litigate whether Dijkstra, the cost weighting, or the blocking logic are computed correctly — `ROUTING_VALIDATION_REPORT.md` already establishes that with a passing self-test and live verification, and nothing here contradicts it. Every item above is about the gap between "the algorithm is provably correct over its inputs" and "a government agency can stake an evacuation decision on those inputs and this UI, live, during a real event" — which is a materially different and, on the evidence above, currently unmet bar. The two highest-priority items (F2: an unvalidated flood model, and D6: unmodelled underpass flooding) are specifically about that gap between correctness and truth, and should be the first two addressed before any claim of operational readiness is made.
